# Moving a Canvas 2D deer scene to photoreal WebGL2

**Move the scene to WebGL2 first, then spend effort in this order: one shared atmosphere function, HDR with filmic tone mapping, Fresnel water that reflects that sky, depth haze, and only then the organic subjects.** Canvas 2D cannot run per-pixel procedural shading at 1900x1069, and photoreal sky, water and haze effectively require fragment shaders. WebGL2 is the safe base: `EXT_color_buffer_float` is reported on 99.84% of Android WebGL2 sessions ([web3dsurvey](https://web3dsurvey.com/webgl2/extensions/EXT_color_buffer_float)). WebGPU is a progressive enhancement at best, because Android Chrome shipped it only for Android 12+ with Qualcomm and ARM GPUs ([Chrome for Developers](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-121)). The cheapest realism gains are lighting-consistency gains, not geometry gains: sky, fog, reflections and ambient light should all derive from one model. The expensive part is the deer, where fur, gait and foliage are feasible but unmeasured on phones. The notes contain no Adreno/Mali benchmarks at all, so every performance budget below is a design target to verify on the device, not a sourced figure. Most references are 2001-2020 classics; the notes found almost nothing 2026-specific except Chrome's WebGPU posts and one Codrops grass article.

## Why WebGL2 with a Canvas 2D fallback is the platform answer

The platform decision is mostly settled by what the techniques need. Per-pixel atmosphere raymarching, fbm clouds, a mip-chain bloom and an HDR tone-map pass are all fragment-shader work, and the notes' own inference is that Canvas 2D cannot do them at this resolution. WebGPU's Android story is conditional: Chrome 121 enabled it only on Android 12+ with Qualcomm and ARM GPUs, and Chrome 146 added an opt-in "compatibility mode" that runs on OpenGL ES 3.1 as a restricted subset ([Chrome 146](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/new-in-webgpu-146)). W3C had published it only as a Candidate Recommendation Draft in March 2026 ([Cinevva summary](https://app.cinevva.com/news/2026-03-10-chrome-146-webgpu-compatibility), secondary). The one thing WebGPU would buy is compute shaders for grass culling, and the Codrops author who built a WebGPU grass world reported WebGL's draw-call and CPU limits as the constraint ([Codrops, Apr 2026](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/21/false-earth-from-webgl-limits-to-a-webgpu-driven-world/)). A second code path in a single HTML file is not worth that gain. Recommended structure: raw WebGL2 (roughly 100-150 lines of setup, per the notes' estimate, which is unsourced), with the existing Canvas 2D scene kept as the fallback when `getContext('webgl2')` returns null. A library such as three.js 0.186.1 is available pinned on cdnjs ([cdnjs](https://cdnjs.com/libraries/three.js)), but for a full-screen quad plus a few offscreen passes it adds a network dependency and hundreds of KB (size unverified) for little benefit.

The performance picture is the weakest part of the evidence. The 1900x1069 target is about 2.03 MP, and a phone's native framebuffer is roughly 1.1-2 MP depending on layout (the notes' arithmetic, an inference). The one practical rule found is to scale the canvas to 0.5-0.7 of devicePixelRatio, which cuts fragments 55-75% at DPR 3, with tiers near 70%, 50% and 35% ([abratabia](https://abratabia.com/mobile-browser-performance/webgl-mobile-performance.php)). That source is a single-author site, and its claim that `mediump` speeds Adreno/Mali fragment work by 30-50% is an unverified vendor claim. MDN supplies the safer rules: make extensions optional, avoid blocking calls such as `getError` and `readPixels` mid-frame, use `texStorage` and `invalidateFramebuffer`, and use `KHR_parallel_shader_compile` ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/WebGL_API/WebGL_best_practices)). Context loss is documented: call `preventDefault()` on `webglcontextlost` and rebuild everything on restore ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/HTMLCanvasElement/webglcontextlost_event)). Everything else is design inference: an adaptive governor on a smoothed frame time (step the scale down above about 20 ms, up slowly below about 14 ms, in quantized steps such as 0.5, 0.625, 0.75, 1.0), a 30 fps cap as a thermal fallback, `antialias:false, depth:false, alpha:false`, and an RGBA16F fallback ladder down to RGBA8 with in-shader tone mapping. No thermal-throttle timing, Compute Pressure API guidance or per-GPU ALU budget was found. Because the budget is unknown, treat the ray-march step counts, fbm octaves and blade counts as knobs the governor can turn.

## One atmosphere function drives sky, light, haze and clouds

The strongest recurring theme is consistency. Compute the sky once as a function of view direction and reuse it for the sky itself, distance haze color, water reflection and ambient light. Of the sky models surveyed, Preetham paints high-turbidity sunsets a uniform orange-yellow, and Hosek-Wilkie fixes that but needs large coefficient tables and, by its own authors' account, has no solar disc and no dusk below 0 degrees elevation ([Hosek & Wilkie 2012](https://cgg.mff.cuni.cz/projects/SkylightModelling/HosekWilkie_SkylightModel_SIGGRAPH2012_Preprint.pdf)). Those limits matter for a dawn scene. The notes therefore favor a single-scattering Rayleigh plus Mie raymarch, optionally baked to a small sky texture, with the sun disc and near-sun glow added analytically (inference). The reference parameters are Rayleigh beta of about 3.8e-6, 13.5e-6, 33.1e-6 per meter (the note's own reference table; it lists 5.8e-6 for the red channel in text), a Mie phase with g of about 0.76, 16 view samples and 8 light samples ([Scratchapixel](https://www.scratchapixel.com/lessons/procedural-generation-virtual-worlds/simulating-sky/simulating-colors-of-the-sky.html)). That page is internally inconsistent, including a 100x discrepancy in the Mie coefficient between text and code, so verify the constants before use. A cheaper alternative is the Three.js Sky approximation, whose constants (Rayleigh zenith length 8.4e3 m, Mie 1.25e3 m, Henyey-Greenstein g 0.8, an earth-shadow fade of sun intensity) are documented ([three.js Sky.js](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mrdoob/three.js/dev/examples/jsm/objects/Sky.js)). Hillaire's production LUT method was not retrieved (the fetch failed), so no claims about it are made beyond its existence. The notes also infer that single scattering alone looks too dark and saturated at sunrise, and recommend a multiple-scattering fudge (ambient lift, blue tint toward the horizon); no source tests this.

Lighting then follows a three-light rig in linear space: a warm sun (about `1.64, 1.27, 0.99`), a cool sky fill (`0.16, 0.20, 0.28`, scaled by `0.5+0.5*N.y`) and a warm bounce (`0.40, 0.28, 0.20`) from the horizontal opposite the sun, with per-channel shadow powers such as 1.0/1.2/1.5 for warm penumbrae, fog applied after lighting, and gamma last ([Quilez, outdoor lighting](https://iquilezles.org/articles/outdoorslighting/)). The notes add that the sun color should come from the same atmospheric transmittance at the sun's elevation, so sun light, haze and cloud undersides stay consistent; this is physical reasoning, not a cited recipe. Haze is nearly free: `f = 1-exp(-b t)`, a sun-direction color blend (`pow(max(dot(rd,sun),0), 8)` between bluish and yellowish), and a closed-form height-fog integral ([Quilez, fog](https://iquilezles.org/articles/fog/); [Wenzel 2006](https://advances.realtimerendering.com/s2006/Wenzel-Real-time_Atmospheric_Effects_in_Games.pdf)). Applied to each ridge layer before bloom and tone mapping, with a low-scale-height mist layer over the lake, it is the biggest single depth cue available (inference: no perceptual study ranks depth cues). Clouds are the most optional item. A thin slab with 8-24 steps, 4-5 fbm octaves with detuned lacunarity and rotated octaves ([Quilez, fbm](https://iquilezles.org/articles/fbm/)), domain warping ([Quilez, warp](https://iquilezles.org/articles/warp/)) and Beer's-law lighting with a Henyey-Greenstein phase is the plan. The one performance datapoint is desktop: 8 jittered steps at half resolution took 7.5 ms against 297.7 ms for 128 full-resolution steps on a GTX 1080 ([arXiv 1609.05344](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1609.05344)). Dual-lobe phase values, god-ray sources and cloud shadows on terrain are unsourced gaps.

Water follows from the same function. Reflect the sky function along a ray perturbed by the ripple normal, weight with Fresnel (Schlick form with F0 about 0.02, which the notes mark as general knowledge, not a fetched source), and darken the body color with depth. Sun glitter has a published geometry: its width-to-length ratio equals the sine of the sun's elevation, so a low dawn sun produces a long narrow road of glitter, and slope statistics are near-Gaussian ([NOAA PSL](https://psl.noaa.gov/outreach/education/science/glitter)). Gerstner waves with a steepness limit are the standard ripple model ([GPU Gems ch. 1](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems/part-i-natural-effects/chapter-1-effective-water-simulation-physical-models)), but for a calm lake the note's inference is 3-6 sine terms plus scrolling noise normals, fading amplitude with distance so the surface flattens toward the horizon and does not shimmer. Mirroring the mountains by evaluating the same heightfield with the reflected ray, blurring reflections with distance from their base, and shoreline foam are unsourced inferences. Screen-space reflections and FFT oceans were not researched.

## The deer and vegetation are where cost and uncertainty concentrate

Organic subjects have the best-documented techniques and the weakest evidence for phones. For fur, shell texturing (typically 16 layers, LOD to 8 or 4) with fins at silhouettes is the classic method ([Lengyel et al. 2001](https://people.csail.mit.edu/ericchan/bib/pdf/p227-lengyel.pdf)). Shading uses Kajiya-Kay with Scheuermann's improvements: tangent shifted by a noise texture to break up the highlight, a primary and a tighter secondary lobe, diffuse remapped to 0.25-1.0, and per-vertex AO ([Scheuermann 2004](https://web.engr.oregonstate.edu/~mjb/cs557/Projects/Papers/HairRendering.pdf)). Epic's Marschner-style real-time hair says shadows are "absolutely vital" and notes most games still use Kajiya-Kay ([Karis 2016](https://blog.selfshadow.com/publications/s2016-shading-course/karis/s2016_pbs_epic_hair.pdf)); a 2023 Marschner approximation costs about 2x Kajiya-Kay on an RTX 3080 and was not evaluated on mobile ([arXiv 2302.06468](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2302.06468)). The notes' recommendation, which is inference, is a hybrid: an opaque body with a procedural flow-aligned coat pattern and belly/back/rump color gradient, 6-10 shells only on the neck, rump and tail, no fins, a fur-colored Fresnel rim for silhouette softness, and a cheap backlight term when the sun is behind the animal. Shell cost is overdraw, N layers times the animal's screen coverage. No source covers deer coat color or patterns, fur on Android, or rim-light formulas for fur, so the coat must be authored from reference photographs.

Motion matters as much as shading. A walk is a lateral-sequence gait with footfall order left hind, left fore, right hind, right fore ([Game Developer](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/art/animating-four-legged-beasts)), and gait timing can be classified by percentage lags between limbs ([Abourachid 2003](https://comptes-rendus.academie-sciences.fr/biologies/articles/10.1016/S1631-0691(03)00170-7/)). Terminology conflicts: a whitetail "diagonal walk" page ([Murray State](https://campus.murraystate.edu/faculty/tderting/mammalogy2001/Tracks/Mammalgaits.html)) uses "diagonal" differently from Abourachid, and that article does not analyze ungulates. The phase offsets of 0.0/0.25/0.5/0.75, duty factors of 0.6-0.7 for walk, planted-foot IK, ear and tail noise and idle state machine are all the notes' inferences. No deer-specific gait data, 3-segment leg IK source or ear/tail motion source was found. Secondary motion (head as a lagged mass, spine flex, tail last) is sourced to the same Game Developer piece.

For trees, space colonization gives natural deciduous crowns ([Runions et al. 2007](https://www.algorithmicbotany.org/papers/colonization.egwnp2007.html)), while recursive branching suits conifers. Foliage shading follows Crysis: wrapped diffuse plus a view-dependent `(E.L)^4` back-lighting term and baked AO in vertex color ([GPU Gems 3 ch. 16](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-iii-rendering/chapter-16-vegetation-procedural-animation-and-shading-crysis)), with layered wind (trunk bend, branch sway, leaf flutter) in the vertex shader ([GPU Gems 3 ch. 6](https://developer.nvidia.com/gpugems/gpugems3/part-i-geometry/chapter-6-gpu-generated-procedural-wind-animations-trees)). Horizon Zero Dawn used cross-plane billboards for distant trees and alpha-tested vegetation with a depth prepass ([HZD summary](https://wiki.kwonsh.duckdns.org/Between-Tech-and-Art--The-Vegetation-of-/Between-Tech-and-Art--The-Vegetation-of-.html), a secondary mirror of the GDC talk). The notes' inferences are that darkening the crown interior is the largest conifer cue, and that leaf textures can be drawn with Canvas 2D at load, preserving the no-external-assets constraint. For grass, a GPUOpen blade model is concrete: quadratic Bezier, 8 vertices and 6 triangles per blade, root darkening `(h)^1.5`, normals blended 25% toward up, Perlin brightness 0.75-1.0 ([GPUOpen](https://gpuopen.com/learn/mesh_shaders/mesh_shaders-procedural_grass_rendering/)). A three.js grass demo reached about 1M instances with per-chunk culling, with fog and terrain-matched color hiding the cutoff ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=83092)). That is a desktop-class result, and the notes' phone target of 100-300k blades is an unsourced estimate. In WebGL2 there are no compute shaders, so culling is CPU-side by chunk with 2-3 LOD meshes. A near-free anchor, a soft contact-shadow decal under the deer, is an inference with no source for mobile contact shadows or SSAO.

## Camera and post-processing sell the photograph

Post is where an HDR pipeline pays for itself. The order is linear scene with fog, optional depth of field, bloom, exposure, tone map, grade, lens effects, grain, sRGB encode and dither ([LearnOpenGL](https://learnopengl.com/Guest-Articles/2022/Phys.-Based-Bloom); [AMD GPUOpen Lens](https://gpuopen.com/manuals/fidelityfx_sdk/techniques/lens/)). For tone mapping, the Narkowicz ACES fit (a=2.51, b=0.03, c=2.43, d=0.59, e=0.14) is cheap and filmic but oversaturates ([Narkowicz](https://knarkowicz.wordpress.com/2016/01/06/aces-filmic-tone-mapping-curve/)). Khronos PBR Neutral preserves base colors with no hue shift ([Khronos](https://github.com/KhronosGroup/ToneMapping/blob/main/PBR_Neutral/README.md)); the GLSL in the notes is a fetch tool's translation, so check it against the repo. AgX desaturates highlights gracefully, but its Blender constants were never retrieved, and the darktable variant must not be passed off as Blender's. Given a dawn scene with saturated orange highlights, the notes' reasoning favors Neutral or a carefully used ACES fit; this is a judgment, and I would test both on the device. Bloom should be a mip chain: 13-tap downsample, 3x3 tent upsample, about 0.04 mix, and a Karis luma-weighted first pass to avoid fireflies, with no threshold ([LearnOpenGL](https://learnopengl.com/Guest-Articles/2022/Phys.-Based-Bloom); [Jimenez CoD:AW](https://www.iryoku.com/next-generation-post-processing-in-call-of-duty-advanced-warfare)). For depth of field, a golden-angle spiral gather with CoC from depth runs in a single pass ([Voxagon](https://blog.voxagon.se/2018/05/04/bokeh-depth-of-field-in-single-pass.html)). Wronski warns that hexagonal bokeh and RGB-offset chromatic aberration in bokeh read as cheap ([Wronski](https://bartwronski.com/2014/04/07/bokeh-depth-of-field-going-insane-part-1/)).

Grain and dithering are separate jobs. A published MIT-licensed grain shader uses 1-4 px cells, a 24 fps frame rate, a hash, a midtone-weighted response `smoothstep(0.02,0.28,luma)*(1-smoothstep(0.72,1.0,luma))`, and default amount 0.08 ([GameDev.net](https://gamedev.net/shaderlab/57-time-varying-film-grain/)). Interleaved gradient noise (`fract(52.9829189*fract(dot(p, vec2(0.06711056,0.00583715))))`) is a cheap dither source ([0b5vr](https://scrapbox.io/0b5vr/Interleaved_Gradient_Noise)), and seeding by screen position alone yields a fixed "dirty window" pattern, so seed with time ([Klint](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/banding-and-dithering)). Exposure should be fixed per sun/sky state rather than auto-normalized; the EV100 formulas are in [Bruop](https://bruop.github.io/exposure/), though the claim that fixed exposure is the main realism lever is an inference. The notes' unsourced guidance, which I treat as practitioner consensus rather than evidence: vignette at 10-25% corner darkening, chromatic aberration of 0.1-0.5 px growing with radius, handheld sway of 0.05-0.3 degrees at 0.3-1.5 Hz, grain at CSS-pixel scale on high-DPI screens, bloom at half resolution, DoF capped at 16 taps, and a ±1/255 dither. Color grading by procedural lift/gamma/gain and split-toning, and veiling glare or lens flare, have no source at all.

## Implementation plan ordered by realism gain per unit of effort

The ranking below merges the notes' own value-per-effort lists. These lists are explicitly judgment, because no perceptual study of realism factors was found. Effort labels are my estimates. The 100-300k blade figure and all frame-time targets are design targets, not measurements.

| Step | Work | Why it ranks here | Evidence quality |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | WebGL2 full-screen pipeline, fallback to the existing Canvas 2D scene, DPR cap with render-scale governor, context-loss handling | Prerequisite for everything; contains the performance risk early | Sourced for context loss and DPR scaling; governor thresholds are inference |
| 2 | Shared single-scatter sky function, with analytic sun disc and glow; reuse for fog color, ambient and reflection | Largest realism jump, because one light model unifies every surface | Constants sourced but one reference is inconsistent; recipe is inference |
| 3 | Linear-light HDR (RGBA16F with RGBA8 fallback), exposure, tone map (Neutral or ACES fit), sRGB, dither | Cheap, and removes the flat "canvas" look | Sourced formulas; choice of curve is judgment |
| 4 | Aerial perspective and height mist on every ridge and tree layer | Strongest cheap depth cue | Sourced fog formulas |
| 5 | Fresnel lake reflecting the sky function, flattening ripples, glitter road, shoreline darkening | Water is the largest surface in the frame | Glitter geometry sourced; Fresnel and shoreline are inference |
| 6 | Bloom (half-res mip chain, 0.04 mix), vignette, grain | Hides CG sharpness and clips the sun and glitter naturally | Sourced bloom and grain parameters; vignette values unsourced |
| 7 | Vegetation shading: base-to-tip AO, backlit translucency, low-frequency color variation, one contact-shadow decal under the deer | Largest gain for grass and trees at low geometry cost | Sourced AO and translucency patterns; decal is inference |
| 8 | Instanced grass blades with 2-3 LODs and wind, matched to terrain color | Big gain for the foreground, but needs a fill-rate and blade-count budget measured on the phone | Blade model sourced; phone counts unsourced |
| 9 | Deer: procedural coat, Kajiya-Kay two-lobe highlight, limited shells, walk cycle with correct footfall order and idle behaviors | Highest effort and the main uncanny-valley risk, so it comes after the environment is right | Techniques sourced; deer data and phone cost are gaps |
| 10 | Low-res fbm cloud slab, bounded depth of field, trees via space colonization or L-systems with billboards | Polish items with real cost | Methods sourced; mobile budgets unsourced |

## Conclusion

The research changes the framing of the task. A "photoreal upgrade" is less about drawing better trees and deer than about replacing separate painted layers with one physically motivated light model, evaluated in linear HDR, and then photographing it with a restrained virtual camera. The environment steps (2 through 6) are well supported by the sources and can plausibly be finished before touching the deer. Fur and gait are well documented in the abstract, but their phone cost and deer-specific correctness are the largest evidence holes.

The sharpest open risks are practical, and they are the things to measure first. No source gave Adreno or Mali frame times, thermal behavior, 16F-target quirks or blade-count limits, so build step 1's governor and a visible frame-time readout before investing in expensive shaders, and let on-device numbers set the cloud steps, shell count and blade budget. Also check the unverified items flagged above: the Scratchapixel coefficients, the PBR Neutral GLSL translation, the F0 of 0.02 for water, and WebGPU's current Android share, which no source covered.
